# Spec: Memory and swap

## Goal
See at a glance whether the Mac is short of memory, which apps hold it, and get it back
without restarting.

## Principle
Macarum shows, the person decides. The overview must be clear enough to choose with
confidence (what holds memory it is not using, what it is, how long it has run); Macarum
never quits, stops or relaunches anything on its own, and every action asks first.

## Where it lives
Clicking the menu bar icon opens a panel just under the menu bar, under the icon when its
position is known and always kept on screen (an NSPopover opened half off screen when the icon
sat in a menu bar hider). A click elsewhere or Esc closes it. ⌘ three times stays clipboard only. The icon gets
a small cut-out dot while memory pressure is high or critical (pushed by macOS through a
memory pressure source, so it costs nothing while idle).

## Contents
- Memory pressure (normal, high, critical) as Activity Monitor shows it
  (`kern.memorystatus_vm_pressure_level`).
- Swap used of total (`vm.swapusage`), with a bar in the pressure colour.
- When pressure is above normal, one line: relaunch the heaviest apps to get memory back.
- The six entries holding the most memory **out of RAM** (footprint minus resident: what macOS
  compressed or wrote to swap because it was not being used). That is what fills swap, so it
  answers "what should I close". Apps add up their helper processes (everything inside
  `X.app`, plus Chrome's temporary `.app.bundle` copy). Programs outside an app stay one entry
  per process and say what they run ("expo start · shop", from the command line), so
  forgotten dev servers stand out. Each row also shows the total and how long it has run.
  Processes of other users (root daemons) cannot be read and are left out. Checked on the
  test Mac: the out-of-RAM total (20 GB) matched compressor (5.8 GB) plus swap (8.4 GB)
  in compressed form; two dev servers left running for 4 and 6 days held 4.3 GB.
- Programs can be stopped (SIGTERM, like Ctrl-C), after the same confirm row.
- On hover, regular apps show Relaunch (quit, wait up to 30 s, open again in the background)
  and Quit (a normal quit; the app may ask to save). Both ask first in the row ("Relaunch
  Google Chrome?" with Cancel), so a stray click never closes an app.
- macOS does not report swap per app; out of RAM is the closest honest measure (it mixes
  compressed memory and swap).
- "Free Cached Memory…" runs `/usr/sbin/purge` through the standard administrator password
  prompt. It drops cached files; it does not empty swap.
- Below: Clipboard History (with its trigger), Pause/Resume History, Settings…, Quit.

## Idle label
A row gets a quiet grey "Idle 3 h" / "Idle 4 days" capsule in its subtitle when it has done
no real work for at least an hour. It is only a label: nothing is ever closed because of it,
and Quit, Stop and Relaunch keep their confirm rows.
- Measure: CPU time used (user plus system, `proc_pid_rusage`; the counters are machine
  ticks, 125/3 ns on Apple silicon, checked against `ps`). A process is active when it uses
  more than 2 s of CPU within one window of about five minutes (270 s), idle otherwise.
- Why 2 s: measured on an Apple silicon Mac (2026-11-07, 30 s samples over 10 minutes), two Expo
  dev servers left untouched for days used at most 0.61 and 0.81 s per window from their
  timers, so 0.5 s would never call them idle; apps in use ran 10 to 35 s per window.
- Each process is remembered by pid plus start time, so a new process that gets a reused
  pid starts with no history. Processes that exit are forgotten.
- An app (everything in its bundle) is idle only when all its processes are; it is idle
  since the latest work any of them did. One busy helper means no label.
- Never a guess: no label until Macarum itself has watched for an hour (history is not kept
  across launches), and none for a process started less than an hour ago. A process seen
  idle the whole time is "idle since Macarum first saw it", a lower bound. The subtitle drops
  "running …" while the badge shows; the badge's tooltip has both times.
- Limit: idle means "used almost no CPU", not "unused" or "safe to close". A dev server open in
  VS Code but untouched shows idle, and so does an app holding a document you still want.
  It is a fact for the person to weigh.

## What macOS does not allow
Emptying swap on demand or turning swap off (needs System Integrity Protection off; not
offered). Swap shrinks when the apps holding it quit or relaunch.

## Budget
Sampling runs every 2 s only while the popover is open (about 600 processes, a few ms), and
stops when it closes. For the idle label, CPU times alone (no paths, arguments or memory)
are read every 5 minutes in the background, panel open or not.
